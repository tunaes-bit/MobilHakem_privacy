1 Signing in is required: Google on Android, Apple on iPhone

You need to sign in to start a debate, to receive the welcome gift, to buy stars and to hire a counsel: with Google on Android, with Apple on iPhone. The app still opens without signing in; you can read the rules and the consent screen, but tapping Start asks you to sign in. Your time is held against your account rather than the phone: it follows you to a new device, and deleting and reinstalling the app does not reset it.

On iPhone you sign in with Apple, and Apple is not asked for your name or your email address. Apple passes our authentication provider (Firebase Authentication) only an anonymous identifier specific to this app; your account record on iPhone holds no name, email address or photo. There is no Google sign-in on iPhone. An account opened with Google on Android and one opened with Apple on iPhone are two separate accounts: stars, debate history and purchases do not move between them. When you delete your account on iPhone, the permission you gave to Sign in with Apple is revoked with Apple as well.

The only thing our server uses when you sign in is the anonymous account identifier that corresponds to your Google account (the Firebase uid) — an opaque string that does not identify you and tells us neither your name nor your address. At sign-in Google passes your name, email address and the address of your profile picture to our authentication provider (Firebase Authentication) — Google's own consent screen tells you so. Those three items sit in your account record, are used for no function at all, and are deleted together with your account; they are never sent to our debate server. Your email address is read only to show you, on your own phone's screen, which account is connected. The account identifier answers exactly one question for us: which account your remaining time belongs to.

A device ID is also used, and it is separate from the account identifier: a randomly generated, non-personal string, stored on your device and sent with requests to our server. It has two jobs: limiting abuse (recognising repeated requests from the same device) and recognising a reconnection to an interrupted debate. Your time is written against your account, not against it. It is not a hardware identifier; clearing the app's data resets it (it can come back on an installation restored from a backup). If purchased time accumulated on this phone in an earlier version that you used without signing in, it is moved to your account at your first sign-in. Usage statistics are collected with a random app-specific identifier and, on Android, your phone's advertising identifier (section 5).

You can sign out at any time from within the app. While signed out you cannot start a new debate; your time is not deleted — it stays on your account and comes back when you sign in again.

You can delete your account from inside the app: tap the menu icon at the top left, open My account at the bottom of the drawer and choose Delete account at the bottom of that page. Deletion cannot be undone and permanently removes everything attached to your account — including your remaining and purchased time and your entire debate history; afterwards you can no longer start a debate in the app. The only record tied to your identity that we keep after deletion is this: how much of your free allowance you had used, how many share rewards you had received and the fact that you had received the welcome gift, stored against a salted one-way hash (SHA-256) of your Google or Apple account identifier for at most 40 days, after which it is deleted automatically. Its purpose is to stop the free allowance and the welcome gift from being claimed again by deleting the account and creating it again with the same account; that record contains no email address, no name and no account identifier, cannot be reversed, and is used for no other purpose. Technical remnants that cannot be linked to your identity (the hash of the purchase token, server access logs, aggregate statistics) and their retention periods are listed on the data deletion page. For details, or to request deletion without installing the app, see Account and data deletion. You can also request it at the address in section 10.

2 Audio and transcript are never stored

During a debate the microphone is used only at that moment; there is no background listening. Audio is processed in real time so it can be turned into text. The audio and the line-by-line transcript are never stored under any circumstances — not on the device, not on our servers, not in any cloud. These two sentences have no exceptions; no setting and no consent changes them.

Everything shown on screen during a debate (text, names, live scores) exists only in the device's temporary memory (RAM) and is gone when the app closes.

Exception — Debate History. If you have signed in with Google, the verdict itself is saved to your account after the debate ends: the date, the topic, the category, the percentages, the judge's reasoning, the per-speaker analysis, the speakers' names (if anyone said their name during the debate) and, if counsel was hired, their submissions. The audio and the line-by-line transcript are outside this exception and are still never stored. These records stay under your control: on the Debate History page you can delete any single debate on its own — the verdict you delete is removed from our servers too — and deleting your account deletes all of them at once. Neither of those requires you to write to us. The consent step at the start of every debate states this storage plainly (section 3).

Optional exception — Voiceprint. If you choose to, you can enrol your voice once: the app listens for 20 seconds, you read the lines shown on screen, and one of them asks you to say your name. Our speech provider turns that audio into a meaningless identifier; that identifier and the name you said are stored only on this phone, encrypted. They are never sent to our servers, no copy of them ever exists in any cloud, and they are excluded from device backup. The audio itself is not stored here either. It does two things: it tells you apart from the person you are arguing with, and it marks which side was you in your history. This step is optional; the app works without it. The other person's voice is never enrolled — if more than one voice is heard during enrolment, nothing is saved. You can delete it in one tap with “Forget my voice” on the My account page, and uninstalling the app removes it from the phone.

Optional exception — Live Hearings (Court Before a Jury). Two people may choose to broadcast their debate live to other users; this happens only with explicit, separate confirmation from both of them at every hearing. Your image and voice are transmitted to viewers live — transmitting is not storing: server-side recording is off, past broadcasts cannot be watched, and the moment a broadcast ends nothing of its video or audio remains. The only thing kept is the room's own technical ledger: the topic, duration, viewer count, vote counts and the moment consent was taken. Not the content of the conversation.

Optional exception — Viewer chat. Messages you type while watching a hearing are kept for at most 30 days for moderation review and are tied to your account. This is not the debate's spoken transcript — what is said is still never stored; what is kept is what you typed and published with your own keyboard. A moderation team that cannot read a reported message would be no moderation at all. They are deleted when the period ends, and entirely when you delete your account.

3 Both parties consent

Before a debate can start, the app shows a step confirming that both speakers agree to be listened to. Recording or transcribing another person without their knowledge may be illegal where you live; do not use this app to do that.

That step also states that the verdict is saved. The person across from you never sees the history screen — the verdict is written to the account signed in on the phone — so the consent text states it plainly, every time: “The verdict of this debate — including any name said out loud — will be saved to your account. Not the audio, and not the transcript.”

4 Third-party services

For the app to work, audio and text are sent to the services below for real-time processing only. We do not instruct any of them to retain it:

  • Speechmatics — real-time speech-to-text (European region).
  • AI judge (third-party AI models) — real-time evaluation of the transcript, the final verdict and the counsel's submissions: Google Gemini; when Gemini cannot be reached, Cerebras, Groq or Mistral. Each of these providers commits in its contract not to use the content it receives to train AI models.
  • ElevenLabs — speaking the winner announcement in a natural judge voice. Only the short announcement text is sent (which includes the winner's first name); no audio, no debate transcript and no scores are sent. The text is not retained after it has been spoken.
  • Cloudflare — live hearings only: real-time delivery of video and audio to viewers. The broadcast is transmitted, never recorded.
  • Google Analytics for Firebase — usage counters and, on Android, measurement and install attribution with the advertising identifier (see section 5).
  • Google Play Billing — star-pack purchases on Android.
  • App Store (Apple) — star-pack purchases on iPhone. Apple's signed record of the purchase is verified on our server; so that the same purchase never writes stars twice, a one-way hash of the transaction number is kept for at most 400 days.
  • Firebase Authentication — Google sign-in on Android, Sign in with Apple on iPhone. With Google, your account record holds the anonymous account identifier (uid) together with the name, email address and profile-picture address that Google passes on; with Apple it holds only anonymous identifiers. Our debate server sees only the account identifier among these; when you delete your account the record is deleted in its entirety.
  • Google AdMob — ads, only on Android and only for accounts in the advertising experiment (sections 6 and 12). The data goes from your phone directly to Google and may be shared with Google's advertising partners (the ad buyers bidding for the ad space) so that an ad can be chosen. Nothing from your conversation is passed on.
  • Firebase Cloud Messaging — Android only: delivering announcement notifications to your phone (section 7). Google creates the notification ID; we do not store it.

This processing may take place on servers outside your country, including outside the United States. The consent screen at the start of every debate says that speech and text are processed by third-party services; it does not cover advertising or analytics. Data sent to Google AdMob and Google Analytics is processed on Google's servers, which may be in another country (sections 5, 6 and 12).

5 Usage statistics

We collect usage counters through Google Analytics for Firebase to answer one question: at which step do people give up?

What is collected: which stage was reached (permission, voice enrolment, debate, result), how long those steps took, whether a purchase screen was opened, whether a connection error occurred; which group of the advertising experiment you are in, how many ads were shown and the revenue Google reports for each one (an amount and a currency, nothing else), and your answer to the ad card in Turkey (section 12); plus device model, operating system version, country and an app-specific random identifier.

These statistics are not anonymous. On Android, Google Analytics also collects your phone's advertising identifier, to measure how the app is used and to see which of our Google Ads campaigns an installation came from (install attribution). On Android, versions of the app after 1.51.2 switch Analytics' ad-personalisation signals off; in those versions this data is not used to personalise the ads you see. No advertising identifier is collected on iPhone. You can reset or delete your advertising identifier in your phone's settings. The data is processed on Google's servers.

What is NOT collected: audio, transcripts, speaker names, the debate's topic and scores never appear in any analytics event; nothing from your conversation goes to Google Analytics. Your name, email address or account identifier never enters any statistic. Your verdicts are saved to your account (section 2) — but those records are yours, and they do not enter these statistics.

We have deliberately refused session-recording tools (such as screen replay), because a screen recording would carry the names and live captions on the debate screen to a third party as pixels.

6 Advertising

There are no ads on iPhone; the advertising software never runs there. The rest of this section applies to Android.

Advertising is coming back as an experiment. In the current version some accounts see ads and some do not. Which group you are in is computed from your account and is not stored. The experiment measures what advertising adds to the product; depending on the result, ads stay or are removed again. On a phone that is not in the advertising group, the advertising software never runs.

Where: a full-screen ad before a debate starts and when you open the verdict details, a silent banner at the top of the debate screen, and a rewarded ad you can choose to watch to earn stars. No full-screen or rewarded ad is shown while the microphone is on; the banner has no sound. Buying a star pack does not remove ads.

Ads are never chosen from your conversation. Nothing from your debate is added to an ad request: the topic, the category, names, the transcript and the audio never reach the ad provider.

Ads are served by Google AdMob. To show and measure ads and to prevent abuse, AdMob collects your device's advertising identifier, your IP address and the approximate location derived from it, your interactions with ads and diagnostic information. This data goes from your phone directly to Google, is processed on Google's servers and may be shared with Google's advertising partners (ad buyers) so that an ad can be chosen; it never reaches our servers. Details: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Personalisation and consent — whether ads are chosen according to your interests depends on the country:

  • European Economic Area, United Kingdom and Switzerland: Google's consent form is shown before the first ad, and you can change your choice later with Ad privacy options on the My account page.
  • Turkey: ads are chosen according to your interests only with your explicit consent. It is asked for by the app's own card, not by Google's form. If you decline, or have not been asked, ads are still shown, just not chosen for you. You can change your choice at any time with Personalized ads on the My account page. "Turkey" here means the country of your Google Play account; the app does not use your location or your phone's language for this. Full notice: section 12.
  • All other countries, including the United States: ads are requested as non-personalised.

The banner at the top of the debate screen is requested as non-personalised in every country, even if you have given consent. You can reset or delete your advertising identifier in your phone's settings.

Stars from a rewarded ad: our server writes the star after Google's signed confirmation. Google receives a single-use, meaningless token instead of your account identifier. So that the same view is never rewarded twice, the token is kept for at most 3 days and the record of the view for at most 40 days, then deleted automatically. Neither is written to server access logs.

7 Notifications

There are no notifications on iPhone: the app does not ask for notification permission there and does not subscribe to the announcement list. The rest of this section applies to Android.

On-device notifications: when a debate is paused in the background, or a verdict arrives while the app is in the background, the app shows a notification on your phone. These send nothing anywhere; the winner's name and the score are never written into them.

Announcements: at most once a month we may announce news such as a gift or a new feature by notification. For this, the app subscribes to the announcement list for your phone's language through Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google). The subscription happens when the app is opened or updated, and Google creates a device-specific notification ID for it. We do not store this ID or link it to your account: each announcement goes to everyone in that language; your name, your debates and your account are not used.

To turn them off: in your phone's settings, open the app's notification settings and switch off the “Announcements” channel; debate notifications are not affected.

8 Children

This app is for users aged 13 and over. It is not part of the Google Play Families programme and it is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13.

9 Your rights

Because audio and transcripts are never stored, there is no record of them to access or delete.

What our server does hold: the usage counter tied to your account (deleted when you delete your account), the deleted-account trace (section 1, at most 40 days), server access logs (IP address and timestamp, kept briefly for security), rewarded-ad records (section 6, at most 40 days) and your debate history (section 2).

You do not need to write to us to delete your history. On the Debate History page inside the app you can delete any single debate you choose, and the verdict you delete is removed from our servers too; deleting your account deletes every record at once. You may still contact us at any time about anything else held about your account.

If your Google Play country is Turkey, section 12 describes personalised ads, how to withdraw consent and your rights under Turkish law (KVKK). The record of that consent is kept only on your phone and our server holds none; only the choice itself goes to Google Analytics as a usage statistic (sections 5 and 12).

California residents (CCPA/CPRA). In the past 12 months we have collected the categories described above: identifiers (a random device ID and, on Android, the advertising identifier — collected by Google Analytics for measurement and install attribution, and by AdMob for accounts in the advertising group), commercial information (purchase records), internet activity (usage counters) and audio — the last of which is processed in real time and never retained. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not sell or share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. Advertising returned as an experiment in September 2026 (section 6): for accounts in the advertising group, Google AdMob receives the advertising identifier, IP address and ad interactions to deliver and measure ads. In the United States these ads are requested as non-personalised, so AdMob does not use your data for cross-context behavioural advertising. On Android, versions of the app after 1.51.2 also switch Google Analytics' ad-personalisation signals off, so in those versions analytics data is not used to personalise ads either. You have the right to know, delete and correct, and to not be discriminated against for exercising those rights. To exercise them, email the address in section 10. Making such a request needs no account: we will not ask you to install the app or to sign in in order to exercise a privacy right.

12 Personalised ads in Turkey

This is the notice that Turkey's Personal Data Protection Law No. 6698 (KVKK, Article 10) requires for the consent you may give to ads being chosen according to your interests. Consent is asked for separately, on a card in the app; reading this notice is not consent.

Who it applies to: accounts on Android that are in the advertising experiment and whose Google Play country is Turkey. The app does not use your location or your phone's language to decide this. There are no ads on iPhone.

Data controller: Bizzy Mobil Uygulama Çözümleri Ltd. Şti. (contact details in section 10).

Data processed: your phone's advertising identifier and app set identifier; device and app information (device model, operating system and diagnostics such as app launch time and hang rate); your IP address and the approximate location derived from it; and your interactions with ads (app launches, taps, video views).

How it is collected: the Google Mobile Ads software in the app (Google AdMob) collects this data automatically and sends it from your phone directly to Google; it never reaches our servers. Nothing from your debates — topic, category, names, transcript, audio — is added to it; ads are never chosen from your conversation.

Purpose: letting Google choose the ads you see according to your interests (personalised advertising).

Legal basis: your explicit consent (KVKK Article 5(1)). Consent is not a condition of using the app: if you decline, the app works exactly the same and ads are still shown, just not chosen for you. Even then, Google processes some of the data above to show and measure ads and to prevent abuse (section 6).

Recipients: Google and Google's advertising partners (the ad buyers bidding for the ad space).

Transfer abroad: the data is transferred to, and processed on, Google's servers outside Turkey (KVKK Article 9).

Retention: the record of your choice — the choice (allowed or not allowed), when you made it and the version of the text you saw — is kept only on your phone; it is never sent to our servers and is not linked to your account. It is erased when you sign out or delete your account; your phone's own backup may carry it to a new phone set up with the same Google account. The choice itself (allowed, not allowed or not yet asked) also goes to Google Analytics as a usage statistic, so that we can measure how many people allow it (section 5). That copy is held by Google and is linked not to your account but to the app-specific random identifier and your phone's advertising identifier. Statistics already sent to Google Analytics are not erased by signing out or deleting your account; they are deleted when Google Analytics' retention period ends. If you close the card without answering, there is no choice, ads stay non-personalised and the card may come back after 7 days at the earliest; declining is a choice, and you are not asked again unless the card's text changes. Data collected by Google is kept under Google's own rules: policies.google.com/technologies/retention.

Withdrawing consent: tap Personalized ads on the My account page to change your choice at any time — withdrawing is as easy as consenting. From that moment any ad already waiting on the phone is discarded and later ads are requested as non-personalised. You can also reset or delete your advertising identifier in your phone's settings.

Your rights (KVKK Article 11): to learn whether your personal data is processed and, if so, to request information about it; to learn the purpose of the processing and whether the data is used for that purpose; to know the third parties in Turkey or abroad to whom it is transferred; to have incomplete or inaccurate data corrected; to have it deleted or destroyed under the conditions of KVKK Article 7, and to have such corrections and deletions notified to those third parties; to object to a result against you that arises solely from automated analysis; and to claim compensation for damage caused by unlawful processing.

How to apply: write to the email address in section 10; your application is answered free of charge within 30 days at the latest (KVKK Article 13). Because the record of your choice is kept only on your phone and the statistic in Google Analytics is not linked to your account, we cannot look your choice up from your account or change it for you — the quickest way is the row on the My account page. For the data Google processes you can also contact Google: policies.google.com/privacy. If your application is refused or not answered in time, you may complain to Turkey's Personal Data Protection Board (KVKK Article 14).
