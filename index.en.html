1 Signing in is optional

The entire app works without an account. If you do not sign in we never ask for your name, email address or any identity document. To track your remaining usage we use a randomly generated device ID: it is stored on your device, sent with requests to our server, and held there only alongside your remaining time. It is not a hardware identifier and it is deleted automatically once unused. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app resets it.

If you choose to sign in with Google, your weekly free time doubles. In that case the only thing we store is the anonymous account identifier that corresponds to your Google account (the Firebase uid) — an opaque string that does not identify you and tells us neither your name nor your address. At sign-in Google passes your name, email address and profile picture to the app — Google's own consent screen tells you so. We do not read them, do not process them and never write them anywhere; the account identifier is the only thing that persists. The account identifier answers exactly one question for us: which account your remaining time belongs to. When you sign in, the time left on that device is transferred to your account, so it follows you to a new phone.

You can sign out at any time from within the app, and it returns to working without an account. If you want your account and the time attached to it deleted entirely, you can request that at the address in section 9.

2 Audio and transcript are never stored

During a debate the microphone is used only at that moment; there is no background listening. Audio is processed in real time so it can be turned into text. The audio and the line-by-line transcript are never stored under any circumstances — not on the device, not on our servers, not in any cloud. These two sentences have no exceptions; no setting and no consent changes them.

Everything shown on screen during a debate (text, names, live scores) exists only in the device's temporary memory (RAM) and is gone when the app closes.

Optional exception — Debate History. For users who have signed in with Google and have turned this setting on themselves, the verdict itself is saved to their account after the debate ends: the date, the topic, the category, the percentages, the judge's reasoning, the per-speaker analysis, the speakers' names (if anyone said their name during the debate) and, if counsel was hired, their submissions. The audio and the line-by-line transcript are outside this exception and are still never stored. The setting is off by default. Turning it off inside the app deletes all of your records at that moment, and deleting your account deletes them too.

Optional exception — Voiceprint. If you choose to, you can enrol your voice once: the app listens for 20 seconds, you read the lines shown on screen, and one of them asks you to say your name. Our speech provider turns that audio into a meaningless identifier; that identifier and the name you said are stored only on this phone, encrypted. They are never sent to our servers, no copy of them ever exists in any cloud, and they are excluded from device backup. The audio itself is not stored here either. It does two things: it tells you apart from the person you are arguing with, and it marks which side was you in your history. This step is optional; the app works without it. The other person's voice is never enrolled — if more than one voice is heard during enrolment, nothing is saved. You can delete it in one tap with “Forget my voice” on the home screen, and it is deleted automatically when you turn debate history off.

3 Both parties consent

Before a debate can start, the app shows a step confirming that both speakers agree to be listened to. Recording or transcribing another person without their knowledge may be illegal where you live; do not use this app to do that.

With Debate History on, that step says so. The person across from you never sees the history screen — the phone's owner turns the setting on — so when there is something to save, the consent text states it plainly: “The verdict of this debate (not the audio, not the transcript) will be saved to your account.”

4 Third-party services

For the app to work, audio and text are sent to the services below for real-time processing only. We do not instruct any of them to retain it:

  • Speechmatics — real-time speech-to-text (European region).
  • AI judge (LLM) — real-time evaluation of the transcript (live scoring: Groq; final analysis: Google Gemini).
  • ElevenLabs — speaking the winner announcement in a natural judge voice. Only the short announcement text is sent (which includes the winner's first name); no audio, no debate transcript and no scores are sent. The text is not retained after it has been spoken.
  • Google Analytics for Firebase — anonymous usage counters only (see section 5).
  • Google Play Billing — extra-time purchases.
  • Firebase Authentication — only if you choose to sign in with Google. Google performs the sign-in; only the anonymous account identifier (uid) reaches us.

This processing may take place on servers outside your country, including outside the United States, and is disclosed on the consent screen inside the app.

5 Usage statistics

We collect anonymous usage counters through Google Analytics for Firebase to answer one question: at which step do people give up?

What is collected: which stage was reached (permission, voice enrolment, debate, result), how long those steps took, whether a purchase screen was opened, whether a connection error occurred; plus device model, operating system version, country and an app-specific random identifier.

What is NOT collected: audio, transcripts, speaker names and scores never appear in any analytics event. Your name, email address or identity never enters any statistic. If you turned Debate History on, your verdicts are saved to your account (section 2) — but those records are yours, and they do not enter these statistics.

We have deliberately refused session-recording tools (such as screen replay), because a screen recording would carry the names and live captions on the debate screen to a third party as pixels.

6 Advertising

This app shows no ads. There is no advertising during free time or during purchased time, and the app contains no advertising code.

Your advertising identifier is not collected, and none of your data is shared with third parties for advertising.

Advertising was removed entirely in version 1.19.0. If you are on an older version, you may continue to see a Google AdMob banner during free time — and your advertising identifier may still be used for it — until you update the app from the Play Store.

7 Children

This app is for users aged 13 and over. It is not part of the Google Play Families programme and it is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13.

8 Your rights

Because audio and transcripts are never stored, there is no record of them to access or delete.

What our server does hold: the usage counter tied to the random device ID (automatically deleted after a short period), server access logs (IP address and timestamp, kept briefly for security), and only if you turned the setting on, your debate history.

You do not need to write to us to delete your history. Turning the switch off on the Debate History page deletes every record at that moment, and deleting your account does the same. You may still contact us at any time about anything else held about your device ID.

California residents (CCPA/CPRA). In the past 12 months we have collected the categories described above: identifiers (a random device ID), commercial information (purchase records), internet activity (anonymous usage counters) and audio — the last of which is processed in real time and never retained. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not sell or share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. Up to version 1.18.0 an advertising identifier was made available to Google AdMob to serve ads, which some laws treat as "sharing"; advertising was removed in 1.19.0 and no advertising identifier is collected by that version or later. You have the right to know, delete and correct, and to not be discriminated against for exercising those rights. To exercise them, email the address in section 9 — no account is required and we will not ask you to create one.
